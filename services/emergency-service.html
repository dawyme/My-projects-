What Qualifies as an HVAC/R Emergency?

Not every HVAC issue requires emergency service, but these situations definitely do:

❄️ Complete Loss of Cooling

When your air conditioning completely stops working during hot weather, especially if it poses a health risk to vulnerable individuals (elderly, infants, those with medical conditions).

🔥 Heating Failure in Cold Weather

When your heating system fails during unusually cold periods, creating unsafe indoor temperatures.

💧 Major Water Leaks

Significant water leakage from HVAC equipment that could cause property damage, electrical hazards, or mold growth.

⚡ Electrical Issues

Smoke, burning smells, sparks, or tripped breakers from HVAC equipment indicating potential fire hazards.

🧊 Refrigeration Failure

When refrigeration systems fail, risking food spoilage, medication loss, or other temperature-sensitive product damage.

💨 Gas Leaks or Odors

Any smell of natural gas or refrigerant leaks that could pose health or safety risks.

🏥 Critical Facility Issues

HVAC problems in hospitals, nursing homes, or other facilities where temperature control is critical for patient care.

How to Prepare for HVAC/R Emergencies

Being prepared can help minimize damage and speed up resolution when emergencies occur.

Know Your System

Keep basic information about your HVAC and refrigeration systems readily available.

  • Brand, model, and age of equipment
  • Service history and past problems
  • Location of shut-off valves and switches
  • Any known issues or concerns...

Regular Maintenance

The best way to prevent emergencies is through regular, professional maintenance.

Consider our maintenance plans to keep your systems in top condition.

Clear Access

Ensure emergency technicians can easily access your equipment.

  • Keep area around outdoor units clear
  • Ensure service doors and panels are accessible
  • Know where circuit breakers and disconnects are located
  • Have ladders or equipment ready if needed for rooftop units

Document Issues

If you notice problems developing, document them to help with diagnosis.

  • Take photos or videos of issues
  • Note when problems occur and what makes them better/worse
  • Keep a log of unusual sounds, smells, or performance changes
  • Monitor energy bills for sudden increases
